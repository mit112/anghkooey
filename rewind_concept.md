# Rewind — Product Concept & Specification

> *Capture anything. Remember it forever. Zero-friction spaced repetition.*

---

## 1. The Vision & Core Problem

Spaced repetition is a superpower. It is the most scientifically proven method for moving information from short-term memory to permanent, lifetime recall.

Yet, **95% of people who try spaced repetition drop it within a month.** Why?

1. **The Card Creation Tax:** Writing good flashcards is a highly skilled, tedious chore. To make a good card, you have to read a text, distill the core concept, format it as a clear Q&A, and type it out. Most users spend 80% of their time *writing cards* and only 20% *actually learning*.
2. **The "999+ Due" Backlog Dread:** If you miss two days of reviews in traditional apps (like Anki), your due cards stack up exponentially. You open the app, see a mountain of 600 cards due, feel a wave of psychological dread, and close the app. The system punishes life getting in the way.
3. **Friction on Mobile:** The best mobile apps are purely for *reviewing*. Creating cards on a phone keyboard is a miserable experience.
4. **Privacy Concerns:** Your personal knowledge, reading habits, and private notes shouldn't sit on a corporate cloud server or be used to train commercial LLMs.

**Rewind** solves these three friction points by combining **multimodal mobile capture**, **instant on-device AI card generation**, and **anti-burnout spaced repetition** into a beautiful, native iOS experience.

---

## 2. Core Philosophy

### 1. Capture at the Moment of Friction
You shouldn't have to wait until you are "studying" to make a card. You should make a card the exact second you encounter an interesting idea—whether you are walking, reading a physical book, browsing Safari, or listening to a podcast. Capture is the trigger; AI handles the rest.

### 2. Cognitive Offloading (AI is the Editor)
Humans are excellent at recognizing interesting information but terrible at structuring it for optimal active recall. Rewind's AI behaves as a master editor: it takes your raw, messy, or conversational input and instantly structures it into clean, crisp, active-recall prompts.

### 3. Grace-First Spaced Repetition
Life is chaotic. A learning tool should accommodate human schedules, not demand absolute adherence. Rewind treats consistency as a goal but handles backlogs with grace, adapting the review schedule dynamically so the user never faces "backlog trauma."

### 4. Zero Cloud Footprint
Your memory belongs to you. Rewind works fully offline. Card generation, OCR, transcription, and scheduling run locally on the Apple Neural Engine. Your data stays on your device (and optional private iCloud sync).

### Pillar 1: Multimodal Capture (The Input Channels)

We make capturing ideas as fast as taking a screenshot.

* **Share Sheet Extension:** Browsing Safari, Apple Books, Kindle, or Medium? Highlight a passage, tap Share → "Send to Rewind." The raw text is instantly queued for AI generation.
* **Camera OCR (Vision):** Reading a physical textbook, paper handout, or whiteboard? Snap a photo inside Rewind. The system extracts the text, cleans up formatting errors, and turns it into cards.
* **Voice Quick-Jot (Siri / Voice Memo):** Walking, driving, or washing dishes? *"Hey Siri, tell Rewind that the Pareto Principle means 80% of results come from 20% of effort."* Rewind transcribes it on-device and creates a card.
* **Ambient Clipboard Capture:** When you open the app, if your clipboard contains text, Rewind asks: *"Create cards from clipboard?"*

---

### Pillar 2: On-Device AI Card Engine (The Local Brain)

A ~3B parameter model on the Neural Engine is extremely fast at structured text distillation. Using iOS 26's `@Generable` capabilities, it instantly converts raw text into three card types:

#### 1. The Classic Q&A (Active Recall)
* *Input:* "Mitochondria are double-membraned organelles found in most eukaryotic organisms. They generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy."
* *AI Output:* 
  * **Q:** What is the primary chemical energy currency generated by mitochondria?
  * **A:** Adenosine triphosphate (ATP).

#### 2. The Cloze Deletion (Fill-in-the-Blank)
* *AI Output:* Eukaryotic cells rely on the **[mitochondria]** to generate the majority of their ATP supply.

#### 3. Contextual Application (Deep Learning)
* *Input:* "Confirmation bias is the tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information in a way that confirms one's prior beliefs."
* *AI Output:* 
  * **Q:** You are researching a controversial topic and only click links that support your existing opinion. What cognitive bias is this an example of?
  * **A:** Confirmation bias.

#### 🧠 The Mnemonic Generator
For every generated card, the AI provides a optional **"Hint"** containing a mnemonic, visual association, or analogy:
* *Analogy:* "Think of ATP as the cell's fully charged rechargeable battery, and ADP as the depleted battery waiting to be plugged back into the mitochondrial generator."

---

### Pillar 3: The Study & Review Experience

Traditional flashcards feel like homework. Rewind makes them feel fluid, physical, and tactile.

* **Swipe-to-Grade (Tactile Interface):** Instead of tapping tiny buttons at the bottom of the screen, we use intuitive swipe gestures (Tinder-style) for rapid grading:
  * **Swipe Left:** "Forgot / Hard" (Short interval)
  * **Swipe Right:** "Remembered / Good" (Standard interval)
  * **Swipe Up:** "Easy" (Long interval)
  * **Swipe Down:** "Oops, edit this card"
* **Widget Studying (Micro-Reviews):** Interactive iOS widgets on your Home Screen or Lock Screen. You can review 2 or 3 due cards directly inside the widget throughout the day without even opening the app. Excellent for packing study habits into tiny gaps of time.
* **Sketchpad (Active Recall Validation):** For math, chemistry, language characters, or complex diagrams—swipe up a canvas and draw your answer with your finger or Apple Pencil to compare it physically with the card back.

---

### Pillar 4: The Anti-Burnout Grace Suite

Traditional spaced repetition is scientifically perfect but behaviorally hostile. If you drop off, the backlog crushes you. Rewind introduces three "Grace Mode" features:

1. **The Backlog Cushion (Adaptive Pacing):** If you have over 100 cards due, you can enable "Cushion Mode." The app hides the scary backlog number and lets you review in dynamic batches of 15. The FSRS algorithm secretly redistributes the overdue cards smoothly over the next 5 days, so you still learn them but never feel overwhelmed.
2. **Auto-Freeze (Vacation Mode):** Going away for a weekend or dealing with exams? Toggle "Freeze." The review dates are shifted forward, freezing your progression so you return to a clean plate.
3. **No-Guilt UI:** The app completely avoids clinical or shame-inducing language. There are no red badges on the app icon showing "142 due" unless explicitly enabled.

## 5. The Science: FSRS-6 Integration

Rewind uses **FSRS-6** (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler), the state-of-the-art open-source algorithm:
* **The Logic:** Unlike the legacy SM-2 algorithm (which only considers intervals), FSRS measures memory in three metrics: **Difficulty** (how hard it is), **Stability** (how long it takes to forget), and **Retrievability** (probability of recall).
* **The Benefit:** It requires **20–30% fewer total reviews** than traditional systems to achieve the exact same level of retention.
* **Personalized Optimization:** Over time, the app analyzes your swiping history (your specific forgetting curves) and automatically tunes the 21 math parameters of FSRS to match *your* unique brain.

## 6. Open Product Questions

These are the strategic choices we need to align on to lock the concept:

### 1. How deeply should we integrate with Anki?
Anki is the king, and its users have massive collections.
* *Option A:* Fully compatible import/export (allows users to export .apkg files to/from Anki).
* *Option B:* Completely walled garden (clean start, focused only on our creation flow).
* *Recommendation:* **Option A (Import compatibility).** Med students are the most active SRS users in the world. If we let them import their existing Anki decks, we immediately tap into a massive, desperate user base who hate the AnkiMobile interface.

### 2. Gamification: Streaks vs. No-Shame
* *Option A:* Traditional Duolingo-style streaks (rewards daily habit, but triggers extreme guilt if broken).
* *Option B:* Minimalist, progress-oriented milestones (e.g., "Total facts committed to long-term memory: 432", "Weekly memory score").
* *Recommendation:* **Option B.** Traditional streaks run completely counter to our "Anti-Burnout Grace Suite." We should measure *cumulative knowledge built* rather than a consecutive daily streak. It fits our "Grace-First" philosophy better.

### 3. Deck Organization vs. Tag-Based Search
* *Option A:* Traditional nested folders/decks (e.g., Medicine → Anatomy → Bones).
* *Option B:* A single big "Mind Library" where everything is tagged by the AI and organized dynamically via smart folders (e.g., a dynamic folder for "Economics" is created by searching cards tagged with #economics).
* *Recommendation:* A hybrid. AI auto-tags everything, but users can still manually group cards into decks if they prefer the visual separation.

### 4. Custom Mnemonics: Text only or AI Image generation?
* *Option A:* Text analogies and mnemonics generated by the on-device model.
* *Option B:* Text + AI-generated image (using local image models if available on the device, like Image Playground / Stable Diffusion).
* *Recommendation:* **Option A for the MVP.** On-device text model mnemonics are fast, free, and incredibly lightweight. Local image generation is slow, resource-heavy, and bloats app size. We can consider images as a post-MVP stretch.

## What Rewind Is NOT

* **Not a note-taking app.** It is a retention app. You don't build a wiki here; you commit atomic facts to your brain.
* **Not a traditional quiz app.** It doesn't do multiple-choice testing or generic trivia. Every card is optimized for spaced active recall.
* **Not an exam-crammer.** It's built for lifetime retention, not memorizing terms the night before a test and forgetting them two days later (though our FSRS scheduler can accommodate exam deadlines).
